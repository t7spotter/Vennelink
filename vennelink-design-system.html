Vennelink design system

Vennelink

Two rings, tied. Ink first, colour where it counts.

01 · Brand book

How Vennelink looks and sounds

Vennelink is two rings, tied. The left ring passes over the right one at the top and under it at the bottom, so they read as linked rather than just overlapping. Everything here follows from that mark. Use flat colors pulled from the rings, round shapes where something is meant to feel held, and a heavy, slightly quirky grotesque that sounds like a person, not a platform.

Content fundamentals

  • Write like a friend who remembers things. Address the reader as "you". Vennelink speaks as "we" only in legal or account text.
  • Use sentence case everywhere, including buttons, tabs and titles. Use people's real names: "Call Maya", not "Reach out to a contact".
  • Be specific about time and set it in mono: "23 days since you talked", "Sunday at 9:00". Avoid vague words like "recently" or "a while".
  • Nudge, don't nag. Write "It's been six weeks" rather than "You haven't contacted Priya in 42 days!". Don't use exclamation marks, guilt or streak language.
  • Don't use emoji in UI copy. People can type them in their own notes.
  • Buttons start with a verb and use two to four words: "Send a note", "Snooze a week", "Pick it back up".

Color

  • Set pages on paper and put cards and inputs on surface. Use mist for sunken tracks and quiet bands, and apricot-soft when a band should feel warm.
  • Set text in ink, secondary text in ink-soft, and placeholders in ink-faint. All three pass AA on paper and surface in both themes.
  • Use action (which is ink) for the primary action. Violet is not the button color. Keeping it off buttons is what keeps the system from looking like every other purple app.
  • Use violet only for selection and focus: the on switch, the selected tab's count, focus-ring and the "due" avatar ring. Set violet text in violet-ink.
  • Use coral for one warm highlight per screen: the unread dot, the "New" tag, a single hero shape. Set coral text in coral-ink, never in coral.
  • Use apricot for warm fills such as avatars and illustration blocks. Put on-accent on top of it.
  • Status colors are success (a teal, kept away from red–green confusion), warning and danger. Always pair them with a word or an icon. Coral is not danger, so never swap them.
  • ring-* and tile-* belong to the mark and the app icon. They stay the same in both themes and are used only through the Logo component.
  • The dark theme is the brand ink turned into a room: paper is near-black violet, and surface is the wordmark's own #1D1740.

Type

  • Set everything in Bricolage Grotesque (sans). It is the wordmark's typeface, and its optical-size axis means one family does both jobs. Big sizes get tight and punchy, and small sizes open up for reading. This happens automatically from the font size.
  • Use display-xl once per page at most. It is the only style that uses the wordmark's −0.045em tracking.
  • Screen titles use title on phones and display on wide layouts. Sections use heading. Names in lists use subheading.
  • Body text is body at 16/24. Keep lines at 65 characters or fewer.
  • Set anything counted or timed in mono (DM Mono): gaps, counts, dates. Use tabular figures in columns.
  • Use 800 for display, 700 for headings, 600 for labels and 400 for body. Skip the light weights.

Layout and spacing

  • Spacing uses a 4px base, space-1 to space-16. space-5 (20px) is the gap between the mark and the wordmark. Use it as the default gap between a control and its neighbour.
  • Phone gutters are space-4. On wide screens, cap reading columns at 640px and card grids at 1120px.
  • Left-align text. Center only empty states and the sign-in screen.
  • Separate list rows with a line hairline, not with gaps or cards.

Shape, borders and elevation

  • Make things round when they echo a ring: buttons, tags, switch tracks and avatars use radius-full. Inputs and notices use radius-md, and cards use radius-lg. Never apply one radius to everything.
  • App icons use radius-tile (52 on 220, from the source).
  • Hairlines are border-hair in line. Control borders are border-control in line-strong, which holds 3:1.
  • Prefer a hairline to a shadow. Use shadow-md only on things that float (menus, a raised card) and shadow-lift only on the app icon or one hero element.
  • Keyboard focus is always focus-ring: a 2px gap in paper, then 2px of solid violet. It is drawn as a box-shadow so it follows the radius.

Motion

  • Use 150–200ms ease-out for state changes. The switch thumb slides and turns into a ring, and pressed buttons move down 1px. Skip bounces, parallax and animated gradients.
  • Turn transitions off under prefers-reduced-motion.

Logo

  • Use the files in Logos. The lockup's wordmark is set as outlines from Bricolage Grotesque 800 at 136px and −0.045em, so it matches the source exactly. Don't re-type it.
  • Clear space on every side equals one ring's inner radius (the hole). The lockup files already include it.
  • Use full color on paper and surface, -ink for single-color and print, and -white or the reversed lockup on dark, photographic or action grounds.
  • The gradient lives only inside the mark and the app icon tile.

Iconography

  • The source has no icon set. Use a 24px grid with a 1.75 stroke, round caps and joins, and currentColor. Draw circles as circles, so the icons sit naturally next to the rings. The icons inside Notice and TextField follow this rule.
  • Use Lucide for anything beyond those few icons, with its stroke width set to 1.75. No filled icons, no duotone, no emoji.

Keeping it un-generic

  • Don't use gradient text, glowing blobs, purple-to-pink page washes or glassy blur panels.
  • Don't use cards with a colored side or top stripe. Show status with a tinted field and an icon.
  • Don't center everything. Don't use icon-in-a-circle feature grids. Don't use Inter.
  • If a screen looks like it could belong to any AI startup, take color out, not in. Go back to ink on paper and let one coral or violet detail carry it.

Logo

Brand

The Vennelink mark, lockup and app icon, drawn inline from the source artwork so the gradient and the woven crossing stay exact.

Two rings, linked. The left ring (violet to lilac) passes over the right ring (coral to apricot) at the top crossing and under it at the bottom. That over-under weave is the whole idea of the brand: two things tied together, not just overlapping. Never redraw the rings as a plain Venn overlap.

Props. variant: "lockup" (default), "mark" or "icon". tone: "color" (default), "ink" (single colour, right ring at 72%, follows ink in dark) or "reversed" (white, for photos and the action colour). height in px (default 32). title overrides the accessible name.

Use

  • Lockup in the top-left of marketing pages and sign-in; mark alone in app chrome and favicons; icon only where the OS expects an app icon.
  • Keep clear space on every side equal to the inner radius of one ring (20 mark units; about 0.28 of the mark's height).
  • Minimum size: mark 16px tall, lockup 20px tall.
  • tone="ink" on print, on apricot or coral fills, and anywhere colour is unavailable.

Don't

  • Don't stretch, rotate, outline, or add a shadow (the icon's shadow-lift belongs to the tile, not the mark).
  • Don't set the wordmark in live text next to the mark; use the lockup, which carries Bricolage Grotesque 800 at the source's −0.045em tracking as outlines.
  • Don't reuse the ring gradients on buttons, text, or backgrounds.

Button

Actions

A pill-shaped button; primary is filled with action (ink), so violet stays rare.

Props. variant: "primary" (default), "secondary", "quiet", "danger". size: "sm" (34px), "md" (44px, default), "lg" (52px). icon: a 20px node placed before the label. block: full width. All native <button> props pass through; type defaults to "button".

Use

  • One primary per view, for the thing the screen is for. Everything else is secondary or quiet.
  • Labels are verb first, sentence case, two to four words: "Send a note", "Snooze a week". No trailing punctuation, no "Click here".
  • danger only for actions that delete or remove, and always with a confirm step in the UI.
  • quiet for low-stakes links inside cards (violet-ink text).

Don't

  • Don't fill a button with violet or a gradient. Hover on primary shifts to violet-ink; that is the only place violet appears on it.
  • Don't put two pills of the same variant side by side as a choice; use Tabs.

TextField

Forms

A labelled single-line input with an optional hint or error message.

Props. label (required), hint, error (replaces the hint and sets aria-invalid), optional (shows "Optional" beside the label; mark the rarer case, not every required field). All <input> props pass through; an id is generated when you don't pass one.

Use

  • 48px tall, radius-md, border-control in line-strong. Focus turns the border violet and adds focus-ring.
  • Labels sit above the field, in label style. Never use the placeholder as the label.
  • Errors say what is wrong and how to fix it, in one sentence: "That number looks short. Check the last digits."
  • Body text in fields is 16px so phones don't zoom.

Switch

Forms

An on/off setting that takes effect immediately; when on, the thumb turns into a ring.

Props. label, description, checked + onChange(checked, event) for controlled use, or defaultChecked. disabled, name, id.

Use

  • For settings that apply at once. For choices that need a Save step, use a checkbox in a form.
  • The on state is violet with an on-violet ring for a thumb. That hollow ring is a deliberate echo of the mark; keep it.
  • Label says what is on, not the action: "Weekly nudge", not "Enable weekly nudge".

Tabs

Navigation

A segmented control on a mist track for switching between views of the same list.

Props. items: [{ id, label, count? }]. value + onChange(id) to control it, or defaultValue. label names the tablist for screen readers. block stretches tabs to fill the width (phones). Arrow keys move between tabs.

Use

  • Two to four short labels. Counts are set in mono and turn violet-ink on the selected tab.
  • The selected tab is a surface pill with shadow-sm. Don't add underlines or coloured fills.

Tag

Status

A small pill of one or two words that labels a person, group or state.

Props. tone: "neutral" (default), "violet", "apricot", "coral", "solid" (a coral fill, for "New" only), "success", "warning", "danger". dot adds a leading dot. children is the text.

Use

  • Neutral, violet and apricot for categories people create (groups, circles). Coral for time-sensitive highlights.
  • Status tones always carry a word. Never rely on colour alone.
  • Sentence case, no icons, no emoji.

Avatar

People

A round portrait or initials disc for one person.

Props. name (required; used for initials, colour and the accessible label), src (photo URL), size in px (24, 32, 40 or 56), tone to override the colour picked from the name ("violet", "coral", "apricot", "mist"), ring to mark someone as due (a violet ring with a paper gap).

Use

  • Initials are bold Bricolage at 38% of the size. Fills come from the soft tokens so the page doesn't turn into confetti; only apricot is used at full strength.
  • The same name always gets the same colour.
  • ring means "due to reach out" and nothing else.

AvatarPair

People

Two avatars overlapped like the two rings of the mark, for showing a connection between two people.

Props. a and b: a name string or Avatar props ({ name, src, tone }). size in px.

Use

  • The second avatar overlaps the first by 28% and is cut away from it by a border-ring gap. Inside a Card the gap takes the card's colour automatically; elsewhere set --vn-pair-gap to the ground colour.
  • Use it where a screen is about the tie itself: a shared history, a reminder for two people, an introduction. For a group of three or more, show a count instead of stacking.

Card

Layout

A container for one person, one reminder or one summary: a title, a mono meta line, optional actions and free content.

Props. title, meta (set in mono: counts, gaps, dates), actions (nodes placed top-right), raised (swaps the hairline for shadow-md; for floating cards only), tone="mist" for a quiet sunken card. children is the body. Other <section> props pass through.

Use

  • surface, radius-lg, border-hair in line, space-6 padding (use space-4 on phones).
  • Default to the flat, outlined card. Raise at most one card per screen.
  • Don't add coloured top or side borders, header bands or gradients. Hierarchy comes from type.

ListRow

Layout

One line in a list of people or conversations: leading avatar, name, a mono meta value, one line of description and an optional trailing element.

Props. title (required), leading (usually an Avatar), meta (short: "23d", "Oct 20"), description (one line; truncates), trailing (a Tag or a quiet Button), unread (bold title plus a coral dot), onClick or href (makes the whole row a button or link).

Use

  • Rows are separated by a line hairline, not by gaps or cards.
  • The coral dot means unread. Don't reuse it for anything else.
  • Keep meta to five characters or fewer so names don't truncate.

Notice

Status

An inline message about the state of something, with an icon, a title, optional text, an optional action and an optional dismiss button.

Props. tone: "info" (default, violet), "success", "warning", "danger" (announced as an alert). title (one short sentence), children (one or two sentences), action (a small Button), onDismiss (shows a close button).

Use

  • A tinted *-soft field with radius-md and the tone's icon. There is no coloured side stripe; don't add one.
  • Titles state what happened: "Note sent to Maya". Errors say what went wrong and what to do next. No apologies, no exclamation marks.
